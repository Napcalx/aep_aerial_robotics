Dead Reckoning
The process of calculating the current position of a moving object by using a previously determined position, or fix, and incorporating estimates of speed, heading and elapsed time.
The Process of dead reckoning involves the navigator starting from a fixed position, identified on a map from a previous fix or based on another reliable reference point. They track direction using a compass, gyroscope, or other instruments to measure the bearing.

How Dead Reckoning Works:
• Gyros give angular rate which is integrated to the altitude 
• Accelerometer, rotated into the world frame with gravity removed, integrate once for velocity and twice for position.
• Pitot + magnetometer gives a wind corrected ground track
• Optical flow with rangefinder gives horizontal velocity.
• Flight controller like PX4 blends the Inertia measurement unit driven prediction with intermittent corrections.

Applications of Dead Reckoning

GNSS-Denied Navigation: Keeps drones flying when GPS/GNSS signals are blocked, jammed or unavailable indoors, underground or in dense urban areas.

Anti-Jamming and Spoofing Defense: Acts as reliable, autonomous fallback position calculator if radio frequency interference attacks and disrupt satellite feeds.

High frequency Sensor Bridging: Fills the gap between slow GNSS updates using fast inertial data to support smooth camera geotagging or LiDAR tracking.

Inspection Drones: Tracking position inside structures such as tanks or fuselage bays, often with visual odometry
